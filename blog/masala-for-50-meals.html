How Much Masala Does a Kitchen Need for 50 Meals?

A starting calculation for commercial kitchens estimating spice quantities for 50 portions and planning batch consistency.

In this guide
  • Practical guide
  • Related reading

There is no universal masala quantity for 50 meals: recipes, serving sizes, cuisine, dish type and blend intensity vary. Use this method to establish a kitchen's own standard.

Scale from a tested recipe

  1. Record the spice amount in a recipe that serves 10 portions.
  2. Multiply by five for a theoretical 50 portions.
  3. Test the scaled batch; large batches may behave differently.
  4. Record final grams, yield, cooking time and tasting notes.

Example planning worksheet

If your tested recipe uses 20 g of a blend for 10 portions, the arithmetic starting point for 50 is 100 g. This is only a scale-up estimate, not a universal recommendation.

Consistency checklist

  • Weigh spices rather than relying only on spoons.
  • Record lot, recipe, batch size and cooking method.
  • Adjust water, salt and chilli independently where possible.
  • Document the tasting result and changes.

For restaurant or bulk enquiries, ask suppliers for pack sizes, ingredient information and batch details.

Formula: target weight = tested weight × (target portions ÷ tested portions). Example: 30 g blend for 10 portions gives an arithmetic estimate of 150 g for 50. Trial the larger batch, since evaporation, vessel size and cooking time change.

Kitchen takeaway: Use a consistent measured method, adjust slowly and record what works for your own kitchen.
