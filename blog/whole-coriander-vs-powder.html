Whole Coriander Seeds vs Coriander Powder

Understand how whole coriander seeds and powder behave in tempering, roasting, grinding and everyday dishes.

In this guide
  • Practical guide
  • Related reading

Whole coriander seeds and coriander powder offer related but different cooking experiences. Whole seeds release flavour gradually; powder disperses more quickly through a sauce or spice base.

Use whole seeds when

  • Tempering or lightly roasting them before grinding.
  • Making a coarse spice mixture with texture.
  • You want flavour released during simmering.

Use powder when

  • Building a quick curry or vegetable base.
  • Even distribution through gravy is useful.
  • Recipe convenience matters.

Can you substitute?

Not perfectly in every recipe. Ground powder has more exposed surface area and may cook faster. Start with a modest amount and adjust to taste.

Storage

Keep both sealed, dry and away from heat. Whole seeds may retain aroma longer, while powder can be more convenient.

Substitution and timing

UseTechnique
TemperingBrief seed infusion in oil
Fresh aromaRoast seeds gently, cool, grind
GravyAdd powder at low heat

When substituting powder for seeds, start modestly, taste and record. Avoid dark roasting, which can turn coriander bitter.

Kitchen takeaway: Use these steps as practical starting points, then adjust to your recipe, ingredients and taste.
