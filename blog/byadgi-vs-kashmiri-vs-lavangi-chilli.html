Byadgi vs Kashmiri vs Lavangi Chilli: Colour, Heat & Uses

Compare Byadgi, Kashmiri and Lavangi chillies by colour, heat, aroma and best use in Indian cooking.

In this guide
  • Practical guide
  • Related reading

These chilli varieties are often chosen for different jobs. The names alone do not guarantee a fixed heat or colour: growing conditions, harvest, storage and processing can change results.

VarietyTypical characterUseful for
ByadgiDeep red colour, generally moderate heat, wrinkled podsColourful masalas, regional blends, gravies
KashmiriOften valued for colour with milder-to-moderate heatGravies, tandoori-style preparations, colour
LavangiUsually notably pungent; potency variesWhen a sharper heat is desired, in small amounts

How to choose

Decide whether your priority is colour, heat or flavour. For a balanced curry, cooks sometimes blend colour-focused chillies with hotter varieties, adjusting gradually and tasting.

Kitchen tip

Bloom powdered chilli briefly in a little fat at low heat or add with liquid to reduce scorching. Burnt chilli can taste harsh.

Does red colour prove purity?

No. Colour alone cannot confirm quality, purity or safety. Use reliable sourcing and appropriate testing and documentation.

Quick takeaway

Choose Byadgi or Kashmiri when colour is important and Lavangi when you want more pronounced heat—but always assess the actual batch.

Make a small chilli blend test

Weigh rather than eyeball: try 4 parts colour-oriented chilli and 1 part hotter chilli as a small experimental blend. Test only a pinch in a finished dish, then record the result. This is a starting experiment, not a universal recipe.
